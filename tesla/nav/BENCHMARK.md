# Offline navigation - benchmark report

Generated by `python3 -m tesla.nav.evaluate --all --seeds 30` at commit `dd77b97`, navigation-parameter hash `8dadcc7f3fa8`, 4410 seeded runs.

**Read this first.** Conditions are split into *clear* (wrong road >= 3 sigma from the route), *marginal* (2-3 sigma) and *ambiguous* (< 2 sigma), judged from the nominal GPS sigma, not from how the navigator performed. They are reported separately and **never combined into one success percentage**: a navigator cannot be expected to tell apart roads that are within one GPS error of each other, and averaging those in would hide how it behaves where it can. Every proportion has a Wilson 95% interval; with 30 seeds an observed 0/30 still allows a true rate up to ~11%.

Reporting `ambiguous` instead of guessing is **not** counted as a wrong road. Persistent ambiguity (an episode longer than 100 m, or still open at the end of the drive) is reported in its own column so that it cannot flatter the other numbers.

Definitions are in the module docstring of `tesla/nav/evaluate.py`. Overrides applied: none (defaults).

## Summary (read with the limits at the end)

**Nothing has been tuned.** No navigation threshold was changed on the basis of this benchmark (see *Parameter evidence*). The code under test is commit `dd77b97`; the three constants I added before any benchmark existed (`RELOC_LLR`, `CONTEST_LLR`, `AMBIG_GIVE_UP_FIXES`) are evaluated below.

**What the numbers say, per ambiguity class - never combined.**
- *Clear* conditions (wrong road >= 3 sigma away): parallel, fork/merge, short connecting roads and one-way scenarios are at or near 0% pooled for every failure measure and detect 100% (intervals shown), with a few isolated single-seed failures at 8 m separation / 2 m noise that are listed under *Remaining failure cases* (D3-D5). The substantial failures are in four places: >= 3-second GPS jumps (D1), a GPS teleport onto an unreachable road (D2), repeated deviations (D3) and the diamond split-and-merge junction (D4) - see *Known defects*.
- *Marginal* (2-3 sigma) and *ambiguous* (< 2 sigma) conditions are reported separately in the per-family tables. In the ambiguous class the navigator detects a deviation in far fewer runs and holds a wrong belief for longer; that is expected where roads are within one GPS error of each other, and it is the reason these classes are not averaged into the clear ones.
- Destination on a disconnected road island: 30/30 runs reported NO ROUTE and diagnosed it as disconnected data 100% [89-100].

## Parameter evidence (ablations; defaults NOT changed)

Each variant is the complete benchmark (147 conditions x 30 seeds) with one constant overridden through `--override`; the baseline is the first row. Tables pool families within a class.

**Verdicts, stated with what the intervals do and do not support**
- `RELOC_LLR = 8` (my provisional constant): **supported.** Disabling relocation raises persistent ambiguity from 6% to 9% (ambiguous) and 2% to 4% (marginal) with non-overlapping intervals, and raises clear-class sustained wrong belief from 1% to 2%. A lower value (5) is worse on wrong belief than 8.
- `CONTEST_LLR = 2` (provisional): the **mechanism is supported** - it is a monotone trade between wrong belief and ambiguity reporting (1: sustained wrong 20% but persistent ambiguity 14%; 4: sustained wrong 30% but persistent ambiguity 1% in the ambiguous class). The value 2 is a **policy point on that trade, not an optimum**; choosing it is a product decision about how costly a confident wrong road is relative to saying 'ambiguous'.
- `AMBIG_GIVE_UP_FIXES = 20` (provisional): **not justified by this benchmark.** 0, 10 and 20 are statistically indistinguishable in every class. It implements the 'never recalculate confidently from a contested track' behaviour, but no scenario here measures a benefit. It stays because removing it is also a behaviour change that the evidence does not demand; this is flagged, not endorsed.
- `SIGMA_POS_M` (6): a **genuine speed/safety trade, not a free win.** sigma = 3 identifies the road faster in every class (ambiguous: 49 vs 78 m, detection 90% vs 69%) but raises marginal-class sustained wrong belief from 2% to 8% (separated intervals); sigma = 4.5 is intermediate with overlapping safety intervals; sigma = 8 is dominated. **Not sufficient evidence to change the default.** (An earlier hand experiment suggested larger sigma was better; it was confounded by the tracker bugs since fixed.)
- `SWITCH_LLR` (4): 3 and 6 slide along the same trade-off; no dominance.

## What this benchmark does not show

- **Synthetic networks only**: straight roads, right-angle and shallow junctions, constant 15 m/s, 1 Hz fixes, simple noise models (iid, AR(1), heavy-tailed, uniform). Real roads curve, junction geometry and GPS error are more complicated, and the Tesla UI GPS has an unknown error model. A pass here is necessary, not sufficient.
- **30 seeds per condition**: an observed 0/30 still allows a true rate up to ~11%%; the intervals say so. Pooled rows have narrower intervals but can hide family-specific failures.
- **Classes are judged from the nominal sigma**, so a navigator is never excused for a failure by the benchmark's own opinion of difficulty - but near the 2 and 3 sigma boundaries the classes shade into each other.
- Settling (60 m) and the +-30 m truth window are judgement calls, stated up front; unsettled raw counts and the identification latency are reported next to them. gps_events use a single separation (20 m).
- Turn restrictions, time-conditional access and real traffic are not modelled. Not run on any real road or in the car.
- The **Romanian routes have not been run**: the sandbox blocks all map hosts and no extract of the area is available.

## Reproduce

```
python3 -m tesla.nav.evaluate --all --seeds 30 --json bench.json --md BENCHMARK.md      # the complete benchmark (~2 min; 147 conditions x 30 seeds)
python3 -m tesla.nav.evaluate --family parallel --filter sep=20 --filter noise=8 --seeds 30      # one family / slice
python3 -m tesla.nav.evaluate --family repeated --filter sep=8 --filter noise=2 --seeds 1 --seed-base 4 --trace   # ONE run (seed 5), per-run detail
python3 -m tesla.nav.evaluate --all --override tracker.SIGMA_POS_M=3 --json sigma3.json       # evidence for a parameter change (defaults untouched)
python3 -m tesla.nav.evaluate --compare bench.json sigma3.json --md compare.md               # variants side by side, per class
python3 -m unittest tesla.tests.test_nav tesla.tests.test_nav_deviation tesla.tests.test_nav_tracker_regressions \
    tesla.tests.test_nav_evaluate tesla.tests.test_nav_known_defects                              # navigation tests (known defects = expected failures)
```
Identical seeds and configuration reproduce identical results (tested). Each record stores the seed, scenario parameters, noise sigma and model, git commit and a hash of every navigation constant; `--json` keeps them all.
